AI doesn't build the wrong thing

It builds what you didn't say.

29 November 2026 · How I build with AI · 2 min read

When something I build with AI comes out wrong, it's almost never because the AI got it wrong.

It misunderstands. And when I look back at what I asked for, it's usually because I was too lazy to type out what I actually wanted. If I keep it vague, it fills in the gaps by itself. It assumes things. Reasonable things, most of the time. Just not the things I wanted.

What vague looks like

A real example. I asked for some agent data to update once a week instead of every hour. It was a simple request, and it did exactly that. What I didn't say was that two other parts of the system relied on that data being fresh. Both of them broke the same afternoon. The AI didn't make a mistake. It did what I said. I just didn't say enough.

Another time I spent a day making our landing page builder simpler. It was good work. Three days later I replaced the whole thing with a plain upload, because what the designers really needed was to send a finished page and have it go live. I had described the solution I had in my head, not the problem they had.

Both times the code was fine. The description was the problem.

The questions I should have asked were simple. Who is going to use this? What are they actually trying to get done? What else depends on the thing I'm changing? When I answer those before I start, the AI gets it right far more often.

Describing the problem is the job now

I think the world is moving towards a place where writing code is less and less necessary. What's needed is someone who can explain the problem clearly to an AI, something that checks the quality of what it builds, and someone who can build AI agents that do the work.

That's where I want to be good. Not at typing code, but at saying exactly what needs to be built, and for who, so it solves one specific problem and is easy to use.

You still need some coding knowledge

None of this means you can skip learning how software works. You need some coding knowledge to make sure that whatever AI builds is working as intended. Not to write it, but to read what it did, to know which questions to ask, and to notice when something that says it worked didn't actually work.

I learned that one when our CRM said "OK" to every lead I sent it, and quietly dropped the phone number from every one of them. Nothing crashed. Nothing looked wrong. The only way to catch it was to go and look.

AI builds fast, and it builds what it's told. Whether that's the right thing still depends on the person telling it. On most days that's me, and on the days it goes wrong, it's usually because I was being lazy.
