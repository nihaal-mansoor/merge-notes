Imagine adding rules to a system that never had any

What I learned from splitting leads between two branches.

29 November 2026 · Honest lessons · 3 min read

When I started building our lead system, I had one simple picture in my head. A lead comes in, it goes to an agent, the agent calls. That was the whole design.

I didn't see the next part coming. The company has two branches in two cities, and leads had to be split between them. And not just split. Specific projects had to go to specific agents, from a specific branch.

So I built that. It took a lot of planning, but it worked. Then I was told some agents wanted to work on leads from both branches.

That one sentence undid my clean picture. Now an agent could belong to one branch but work on projects from the other, and the system had to know, for every single lead, which project it was about and who was allowed to have it.

So I came up with a project reference number system. Every project gets a reference, and every campaign carries the reference of the project it's selling. When a lead comes in, the reference tells the system where it belongs and who can work on it. On paper it was neat.

Where it got hard

The idea wasn't the hard part. Explaining it was.

The reference only works if every campaign has it, written the same way, every time. That meant the people running the campaigns had to change how they set things up. And it was difficult to explain why it mattered to someone whose job is to get ads live, not to think about what happens to a lead three steps later.

The bigger problem was that our campaigns were never really structured or planned. Each one was set up in whatever way made sense on the day. So what happened was... imagine trying to add rules to a system that was run with no rules. Nobody wants to accept them. Nobody wants to change how they work just so things can be more organised, especially when the old way seemed fine.

And to be fair, the old way did work. Up to a point. Leads came in, people called them, deals happened. But it didn't help anyone grow. You can't see which campaign is working when every campaign is named differently. You can't send a lead to the right person if nobody knows which project it's for. The growth was stuck, and nobody could see why, because there was nothing to measure it with.

The funny part is that none of what I'm building is advanced. A lead going to the right person, every campaign tagged with its project, a record of who did what. These are basics that every enterprise company has from day one. Here, they're new, and new things feel like extra work.

What I took from it

The challenge is not coming up with ideas to build. With AI, building is the fast part now. The challenge is figuring out how people will use the things you build, and how easy it is for them to use it.

A rule that makes perfect sense in my head is useless if the person who has to follow it doesn't understand it, or can't see what's in it for them.

I don't have a neat ending for this one, because I'm still in the middle of it. Some days the rules hold. Some days someone finds a way around them, and I go back and make the system a little easier to follow. If I'm honest, that's most of the job.
