Making calls should be easy

How I built a calling app around one sentence.

29 November 2026 · Tools for people who aren't technical · 2 min read

There was one complaint about our CRM that I heard over and over from the sales team. It takes too much time. Updating a lead isn't easy, editing one isn't easy, and when your job is to be on the phone, every extra screen is time you're not selling.

Getting numbers to call was even worse. An agent would ask one of our admins for leads. The admin would print them out and hand them over on a sheet of paper. Then the agent would call down the list and, in theory, update the CRM afterwards.

I didn't want to make that process faster. I wanted to skip it.

So I built a web app, connected it straight to our database, and had the leads pushed to the agents automatically. No waiting for an admin, no printout, no sheet of paper.

One sentence

The whole app was designed with one thing in mind: making calls should be easy.

Every decision went back to that sentence. The main one was the number of taps. From opening the app to calling a lead takes two or three. You see the number, you tap it, you're calling.

What happens after the call is just as short. You pick what happened, add a short note if it matters, and if the person asked you to call back, you pick a day. Next time you open the app, the call-backs that are due are at the top, with your last note right there on the row. Every number shows the person's country and their local time, so you don't wake someone up at 3 AM. And on a phone, everything sits in reach of your thumb.

None of this is clever. It's just taking things away until there's nothing left between the agent and the call.

Simple is the hard version

People think simple is the easy version. It isn't. Adding a feature takes a prompt. Deciding it shouldn't be there takes thinking.

I learned that the hard way with something else. I once spent a whole day making a landing page builder simpler. Three days later I replaced it with a plain upload, because all anyone needed was to send a finished page and have it go live. The simplest version was the one with almost nothing in it.

The people using the calling app are salespeople, not tech people. Their job is to sell. They don't want to learn my system, and they shouldn't have to. If someone needs a training session to make a call, I've built it wrong.

Building the app was the fast part. AI made sure of that. The slow part was deciding what to leave out, and that part is still mine.
